# Verba: demo video script (about 2:05)

For the submitted video only. The live judging demo has its own setup.
Voice-over (VO) runs about 250 words, roughly 150 words a minute, so the app's voice has room to land.
**Every shot is real footage of the real app.** Don't film `?mock`, and keep whatever result the app actually shows. If a read fails, film another take.

### 0:00 Hook (12 s)
**Shot:**
1. Bedside, close on the patient actor with a scarf over their throat. A family member holds the phone up.
2. The patient mouths something silently. The room is quiet.
3. The phone plays **their own voice: "Where's my mom?"**

**Caption** (white on black, 2 s): *They didn't make a sound.*

### 0:12 Problem (15 s)
**Shot:** a slow push-in on the bed and on hands resting still.
**VO:** "Patients on a breathing tube are often wide awake, but they can't make a sound. Today they point at letter boards, if their hands still work, and their families are left guessing."
*(If the nurse calls produce a quote, put it here as on-screen text instead of the second sentence.)*

**Shot:** the Verba home screen ("A voice for someone who can't speak right now").
**VO:** "This is Verba. It reads their lips, and speaks in their own voice."

### 0:27 Give them their voice (20 s)
**Screen recording:**
1. Tap **Add a patient**.
2. Type the name, "ICU, bed 4", and pick **Daughter**.
3. Tap **Upload a video or voice memo**, pick an old family video, and check the consent box.
4. **"Creating their voice…"** appears. Cut here.
5. The sample plays: **"Hi, it's me. I can hear you."** Let it breathe for 1 s.
6. **"Saving their phrases on this phone"** counts up.

**VO:** "A family member adds them, and uploads any old video of them talking. Cartesia clones their voice in seconds, and Verba saves fifty phrases in that voice right on the phone, so it works without Wi-Fi."
**Caption:** *Cartesia Instant Voice Clone · Sonic 3.6 · 50 phrases cached on the phone*

### 0:47 Teach it their lips (13 s, sped up 4–6×)
**Shot:**
1. The Teach screen: **"Ask them to mouth: 'I'm thirsty'"**, with the progress grid filling hands-free.
2. End at normal speed on the result card: **"On takes it didn't learn from, first guess right"**, before and after.

**VO:** "Then Verba learns their lips. They mouth eight phrases three times, the model fine-tunes itself on the phone, and it tests itself on takes it never trained on."
**Caption:** *Personalized on the phone in under a second · honest held-out check*

### 1:00 Confident read (15 s)
**Shot:** over the family member's shoulder, phone screen visible. Put a picture-in-picture of the screen recording in a corner if the phone screen isn't readable.
1. Family member: "Do you need anything?"
2. Tap **Listen**. The lip outline glows.
3. The patient mouths **"I'm thirsty."**
4. The bar fills green above 70% ("Confident"), and the voice says **"I'm thirsty."**

**VO** (one line, then silence): "When Verba is sure, it speaks."
**Caption:** *Face mesh → 40 lip points → 3,840 motion features → neural net · all on the phone, no video leaves it*

### 1:15 Unsure, then a blink (13 s)
**Shot:**
1. The patient mouths **"I'm scared."**
2. The bar fills amber: **"Not entirely sure"**, then "Check with them before it speaks."
3. Family member: "Did you mean 'I'm scared'?"
4. The patient **holds their eyes closed**. The ring fills, "Confirmed by their blink" appears, and the voice says **"I'm scared."**

**VO:** "When it isn't sure, it doesn't guess out loud. It asks, and they answer with a blink."
**Caption:** *Calibrated confidence: "60% sure" is right about 60% of the time*

### 1:28 Phrase board (17 s)
**Shot A (6 s):**
1. A read comes back wrong. Tap **"None of these → phrase board"**.
2. The **"Likely from their lips"** section is on top, with the right phrase in it. Tap it.

**Shot B (11 s, scan sped up 2–3×):**
1. Switch to blink scan.
2. The highlight steps through the sections. A long blink picks **Questions**.
3. Another long blink picks **"When does the tube come out?"**, and the voice says it.

**VO:** "If it reads them wrong, the phrase board opens with its next-best guesses on top. And for anything else, they can reach all fifty phrases hands-free, with just their eyes."
**Caption:** *Blink scanning, a standard method in assistive communication*

### 1:45 Undo + log (10 s)
**Screen recording:**
1. A phrase is spoken, then tap **Undo** ("Undone").
2. Open **Conversation**. The entries show "Lip reading · 84% sure", "Confirmed by blink" and "Phrase board", with the undone one struck through.
3. Tap **Share** ("Copied. Paste it in a message to the nurse.").

**VO:** "Anything can be undone, and every word is saved, so the care team knows what they said."

### 1:55 Close (10 s)
**Shot:**
1. Back at the bedside. Family member: "Is there anything you want to say?"
2. The patient mouths **"Thank you"**, and it plays in their voice.
3. Hold on the family member's face.

**End card:** **Verba**, *Their words, in their own voice.* Small text: *On-device lip reading · Cartesia voice cloning · Cal Hacks 2026*
**VO:** "Verba. Their words, in their own voice."

## Before filming
1. **Phone:**
   1. Open https://verba-voice.vercel.app.
   2. Turn on Do Not Disturb.
   3. Set brightness high.
   4. Use Control Center screen recording with the **mic on**, so the cloned voice is captured.
2. **Patient set up ahead of time** with a cloned voice and **Teach it their lips** done. Film the Teach beat on a second patient, or redo it. The earlier beats must use the same voice as the later ones.
3. **Light:** a lamp in front of the patient's face, slightly below. No window behind them.
4. **The lip reader only knows 8 phrases:**
   - yes
   - no
   - I'm in pain
   - I can't breathe
   - Where's my mom?
   - I'm thirsty
   - I'm scared
   - Thank you

   Every lip-reading beat uses one of these. "When does the tube come out?" is reached through the board only.
5. **The confident beat needs 70% or more and the unsure beat needs less.** Do several takes of each and keep the ones that land naturally. Don't fake numbers.
6. **Record the VO separately** in a quiet room, and duck it under the cloned voice whenever the app speaks.
